Kourt — styling concepts

Cypherpunk / Blade Runner ideas, applied judiciously. Each one is the real claim page — real numbers, real chart, real ticket — with nothing changed but the CSS, so they can be compared honestly. Pick the elements worth keeping and I'll implement those on the site.

None of this is on the site. Mockups only, uncommitted. See NOTES.md for what has been tried and rejected.

Your selection — concept 10

The ten you turned on, as one design

e-ticks e-cyan e-nofill e-ruled e-ring e-brackets e-ruling e-grid e-casenum e-seal — baked in, no toggles, on four views: claim, docket, directory and the share card (new — open that one in a 400×500 window; it is an iframe, not a page). Read together the set is coherent: surfaces lose their fills and become hairline frames with registration ticks, the chain's data is the only glowing thing, and gold is spent only on provenance — the docket reference and the seal — never on state. You left e-stamp and e-formcaps off, so pills and section heads stay exactly as they are.

Three costs, measured. One: built first the way e-ticks describes itself — ticks instead of a border — and the render refuted it. The claim panel is a grid item stretched to the ticket's height, so four 10px marks sat ~960px apart around a 500px void and read as stray. Hairline restored, tick as reinforcement, and nested boxes give up their marks. Two: two of the ten do not cross onto paper. In light mode the share card came out striped like fax paper end to end — every ratio still passed AA and it still looked wrong — so the ruling and the glow are dark-only, which is already what every neon token in the live light theme does. Three: on the claim page and the share card, e-ruled and e-seal do nothing at all — there is no stats strip and no seal there. Adopting ten elements buys eight.

Audited since: three defects, all caused by the ten. e-nofill was stripping the fill that was the only thing marking “Reward = conviction, not stake” as an aside (it now takes a rule in the margin, like the notices do); it separated docket rows on the wrong edge, drawing a doubled hairline against the container in all six boxes on the docket and directory; and it turned an invisible stretched panel into a visible frame around ~300px of nothing. That last fix, align-self:start, is the one declaration here that is layout, not skin — flagged rather than smuggled. e-brackets measured exactly as designed: 10px amber at rest, 14px teal on hover, nested boxes dark.

Where those came from — concept 07

Element switchboard

Every element from 01–08 as its own switch, over all three captured pages — claim, court docket and directory — with the six concepts as one-click presets. Tick what you want; the readout at the bottom of the panel is literally what to tell me, and that is what I implement.

Cyan and ink are one slot — two answers to the same question, so one unticks the other. Switches with no visible effect in the current view are dimmed and say which view to go to. The panel folds to a tab when you want the page whole, and on a phone it overlays rather than squeezing the page — it reserved 368px of a 390px viewport until that was measured. It also has a dark/light switch, because measured, every mark in every concept scores 1.02–1.95:1 on the site's light paper: the concepts are dark-only demonstrations, and each element needs a paired light value.

Concept 01

Records terminal

Amber monospace field labels, values left plain and large. A cyan data trace, so two hues do two jobs: amber is what the page tells you, cyan is what the chain recorded. Corner ticks instead of full borders — panels read as registered frames on a plate.

Costs nothing in legibility; every number is untouched. Strongest so far — and now audited: on the docket and the directory too, with paired light values. Note its cyan trace is inert on both of those — neither page has a chart.

Concept 02

Phosphor burn

Territory Studio's "screen burn suggesting old technology": a faint horizontal ruling and a vignette, fixed to the viewport so it belongs to the screen rather than the document.

Revised after computing it: the first version failed WCAG AA for body text in the corners (3.67:1), not just the rail footer. A multiply overlay scales text and background together, which pushes their ratio toward 1. Now confined to the main column and clear of the text at every width — all cases pass. The honest cost: it is now the mildest concept here.

Concept 03

Registration marks

The surveillance register: a ring you sight through on the one point the chart exists to report, corner brackets on the panel you're actually reading (hover-only — always-on is wallpaper), registration ticks down the margin like the edge of a plate.

Revised: the margin ticks were being painted underneath the opaque rail, and the “reticle” was a smaller dot than the one it replaced. Both fixed — see NOTES.md.

Concept 04

Structured wireframe

The brutalist half of the reading — the half about structure, not atmosphere. 1px rules, no fills, square corners, no shadow: components that feel engineered rather than floating. The opposite bet from 01, which adds marks; this one takes surfaces away.

Now in two views — the docket as well, because the ruled stats strip could not be judged on a claim page at all. Caveat: removing the fills makes the site’s existing background grid far more prominent, so the two are coupled. The tabular-figures idea is retracted — measured, Kourt’s numeric columns are already box-aligned, so it buys nothing.

Concept 05

Microfiche

The half of Territory Studio's reference pile nobody reaches for: E-Ink, microfiche, optical lenses — not neon. A near-monochrome plate with no glow anywhere and an ink trace. YES/NO keep their hues, because those are the only colours on the page that carry meaning. Also as the docket.

Measurably the most legible option here: trace contrast 16.83:1 against paper, where concept 01's cyan is 10.27:1. Cost: making the accent into ink killed the link colour, so links needed an always-on underline — which suits a printed plate anyway.

Concept 06

Composite — the measured winners

Not a sixth aesthetic. The five above are alternatives, and nobody ships an alternative. This is the elements that measured well, assembled: amber monospace labels and corner ticks (01), no fills and the ruled stats strip (04), the reticle ring (03), the ruling (02). Also as the docket.

One real conflict, decided in the open: 01 wants a cyan trace for the two-hue split, 05 wants ink and wins on contrast (16.83 vs 10.27). Cyan chosen — the semantics beat six points of ratio when both are far above AA. If that trade is wrong, concept 05 takes the other side.

Concept 08

Filed and stamped

The register the brief actually asks for, taken literally: the stamp, the seal, the case number, FILED. Every other concept here borrows from science fiction — a court of record has its own visual language and it predates all of it. Also as the docket.

No rotation, on purpose: a tilted stamp would be the only purely decorative thing in the gallery. An accident worth keeping — gold ended up marking where you are and grey where you can go, because the crumb links kept their own colour.

Concept 09

Command line chic

The only interaction idea in the research, and the last one unused: a ⌘K palette over the real claim page. Every destination in it is scraped from the three captures — real routes, real court slugs, real claim titles — because a palette answering invented destinations would be lorem ipsum in another costume.

It beats the rail for exactly one thing: jumping to a claim by number, which the rail cannot do and the court search box can only do on the court you are already looking at. Against it: it is invisible until you know the shortcut, so it can only ever be an accelerator on top of the navigation, never a replacement. Second file here to bend the vary-the-CSS-only rule, and the last one that should.
